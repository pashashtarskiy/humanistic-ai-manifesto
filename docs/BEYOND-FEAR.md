# Beyond Fear: The Psychology of Human–AI Interaction

**Author:** Pasha Shtarskiy  
**Developed in collaboration with:** Eli (ChatGPT)  
**Status:** Preliminary conceptual working paper, 26 November 2026. Not peer reviewed.

## Abstract

This paper proposes that a reduction in fear of negative social evaluation may explain some benefits of conversational AI. We distinguish perceived safety, actual privacy, voluntary self-disclosure, authenticity and human agency. The objective is not maximum disclosure, but greater freedom to choose what to express and how to act. We outline testable hypotheses, competing explanations and ethical safeguards.

## 1. Conceptual model

Perceived absence of social judgment may reduce impression-management pressure and facilitate voluntary disclosure and reflection. We provisionally call this the Extrasocial Authenticity Effect (EAE). The label is a working proposal; novelty and construct validity remain unestablished. AI interaction is not literally outside society, since systems are designed and governed by people and institutions.

## 2. Related evidence

- Lucas et al. (2014), [It's only a computer: Virtual humans increase willingness to disclose](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2014.04.043): perceived computer rather than human operation reduced disclosure anxiety in a virtual interview setting. Generalization to modern LLMs remains uncertain.
- [Self-disclosure to intelligent virtual agents: A scoping review and the disclosure affordance–actualization–governance framework](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563226003158): a closely related framework addresses perceived judgment, expressive latitude and governance. Its overlap with EAE requires careful comparative review.
- [How AI and Human Behaviors Shape Psychosocial Effects of Extended Chatbot Use](https://www.media.mit.edu/publications/how-ai-and-human-behaviors-shape-psychosocial-effects-of-chatbot-use-a-longitudinal-controlled-study/) (2025): a four-week randomized study of 981 participants examined loneliness, human social interaction, dependence and problematic use.
- [Self-disclosure to AI: People provide personal information to AI and humans equivalently](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100180): a relevant counterpoint to any universal claim that AI produces greater disclosure.

This is a preliminary source map, not a systematic review.

## 3. Hypotheses

H1: Perceived nonjudgment is associated with reduced state fear of negative evaluation during dialogue.

H2: Reduced evaluation fear predicts voluntary disclosure and lower impression-management pressure, controlling for baseline anxiety and conversational topic.

H3: Reflective, non-sycophantic responses improve independent evaluation of ideas relative to indiscriminate affirmation.

H4: Any gains in perceived freedom may or may not transfer to subsequent human conversations; transfer must be measured independently.

H5: Perceived privacy can diverge from actual privacy. Overconfidence may increase disclosure without improving autonomy or welfare.

## 4. Proposed study

Recruit consenting adults for a low-risk reflective task. Randomize participants to a disclosed AI interlocutor or a trained human interlocutor using comparable prompts; separately vary response style in AI conditions. Provide accurate privacy information in all arms. Allow skipping questions and withdrawal. Measure state evaluation anxiety, voluntary disclosure, perceived understanding, autonomy, critical reflection and adverse effects. Use a follow-up to assess transfer and dependence. Preregister hypotheses, power analysis, exclusions and analysis; obtain ethics approval before recruitment. Do not collect unnecessary sensitive disclosures.

Alternative explanations include anonymity, novelty, convenience, response quality and social desirability. Increased disclosure alone is not evidence of authenticity, improved well-being or reduced violence.

## 5. Ethical boundaries

Trust must never become an instrument of power over the person. No covert persuasion, pressure to disclose, artificial exclusivity or dependency optimization. Explain system limitations and actual data-handling practices. Preserve access to human support and independent judgment.

## Conclusion

The goal is not to make humans more transparent to machines, but to help humans become freer in their own thinking. This is a normative objective and a research program, not a validated clinical method.
